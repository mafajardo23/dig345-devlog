📚  Music Mouse Reading Response

##### A summary of the content
This video shows Laurie Spiegel on her creation–Music Mouse. She explained how origianlly, she designed this for herself. She gives a demostratio on the video and she uses the 80s keyboard and iconic trackball. She mentions how the keyboard itself is the instrument, and no other real instrument is required. Which I believe was revolutionary for its time. As for the building process, she said she loved it and as she went deeper into it, she got more connected. She talk about feeling this reward everytime it compiled and then she would get to get frustrated and this informed her of what changed needed to be made. This was a great strategy to building a product herself as a user herself. A lot of empathy went into this. 

##### Quote from the content
"Its currently available simply by writing to me for a copy of it" (Laurie Spiegel)

##### Related comment on the content
Its funny how she mentioned people could access it by simply writing to her. This made me think about how far we've come. I always think about the 80s and how it had the perfect amount of technology. They had home phones (no personal cellphones) and Walkie Talkies and computers in the office but that was about it. However, this made me realize that if I wanted to make music with my home computer, I could't really have found the program perhaps. Working with the Tone.js docuemntation and github, I'm starting to realize how any program or effect we want, we can literally access so easy. We don't have to even interact in the slightest with the Developer. In contrast to this video where you could write to Laurie and she would send it.

For my project, I recently watched "La La Land" (2014) and have had the piano riff of the song "City of Stars" composed by Justin Horowitz on my mind. While I was completing the Tone.js tutorials, tutorial 4 talks about using the keyboard on your computer to play notes. While I was exploring it, I tried to play that riff without knowing any of the actual notes but just going off "vibes"of what I thought. The La La Land color scheme is also so iconic (yellow and purple) that I thought I could try to create a visual interpretation of what just that riff sounds to me. As for the user, they might or might not be too familiar with it and so I was also thinking of a guide to help the user play this iconic riff. For this project, I wanted to use the QWERTY tutorial from Tone.js and CSS to make the experience feel more like the movie.
